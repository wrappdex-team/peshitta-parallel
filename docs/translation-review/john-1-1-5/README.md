## Blocking semantic and editorial decisions

1. **mltha / ܡܠܬܐ, 1:1.** Greek `Λόγος` and Hebrew `הדבר` are tentative term choices, not proof of a one-to-one lexical equivalence. Word/discourse/matter and contextual or theological resonances need a qualified Syriac review; capitalizing Greek as a title is editorial. The lexical form's grammatical gender does not itself decide personhood or target pronoun gender.
2. **alha / ܐܠܗܐ and predication, 1:1.** `Θεός` / `אלהים` are proposed translations of the source divine term, not replacements derived from the app's English. Greek articles and Hebrew definiteness cannot be mechanically inferred from unpointed Syriac or its emphatic-state forms. The third clause's fronted divine term is retained in the proposed order, but subject/predicate force, definiteness, identity/quality implications and reader understanding need independent review. Do not infer theology solely from absence of an indefinite article. No YHWH name or explanation has been inserted.
3. **lwth and demonstrative reference, 1:1–2.** Greek together-with and Hebrew with may suppress a locative/relational nuance. `Αυτός` / `הוא` lose some demonstrative force of `ܗܢܐ`; reviewers should consider whether a closer demonstrative is better. Pronoun choices remain proposed, not independently established referential analysis.
4. **Agency, hand and hwa, 1:3.** The selected texts retain hand wording; idiomatic through/by is an alternative editorial strategy. Literal Greek may sound bodily; Hebrew `בידו` can suggest possession rather than agency. Repeating come-into-existence / `נהיה` is a contextual proposal, not a lexical certainty of every hwa occurrence. Decide whether the source idiom calls for a different idiomatic target rendering, without silently deleting the image or implying a source word for create that is not present.
5. **1:3–4 attachment.** Current drafts attach `ܡܕܡ ܕܗܘܐ` backward to the negative clause in 1:3. The Greek partitive and Hebrew `מכל` are interpretive expansion/rearrangement and require particular scrutiny. A forward connection with 1:4 must be considered by a qualified reviewer; digital verse-final punctuation alone cannot settle syntax. Do not import an alternative Greek critical edition's punctuation as proof of Syriac syntax or silently move source words across verse IDs.
6. **Life/light and humanity, 1:4.** Test tense/aspect, the referent of `ܒܗ`, life's grammatical agreement and generic humanity wording. The Hebrew plural agreement is target grammar; `בני האדם` is not a claim that a personal name Adam occurs here. No male-only scope is intended by either target proposal.
7. **adrkh / ܐܕܪܟܗ, 1:5 — unresolved.** Greek `δεν το κυρίευσε` provisionally narrows toward overpowering. Hebrew `לא השיגו` is less narrowed, suggesting reach/attain/overtake with possible contextual interpretation. **The two proposed targets are not yet a semantically harmonized pair.** Reaching/overtaking, grasping/comprehending and overcoming are review hypotheses, not dictionary senses independently established in this work. Greek cognitive `δεν το κατανόησε` or a reach/overtake rendering, and Hebrew `לא הבינו` for a cognitive interpretation, are alternatives to evaluate rather than approved substitutes. A reviewer must decide whether to narrow the sense, preserve ambiguity or supply a separate note. This cannot responsibly be certified here. No target may be promoted while this choice is unresolved.

These unresolved points are intentional review blockers, not quietly approved translation choices. Apparent similarity to familiar biblical wording does not validate a proposal or establish an ancient target source. No comparative target-edition wording was copied into this packet.
